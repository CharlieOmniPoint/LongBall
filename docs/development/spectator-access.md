# Spectator invitations and passcodes

Owner-requested extension, 24 October 2026. This changes the earlier restriction that only Players and Managers could issue spectator invitations. Any active user may issue a spectator invitation in their current authorised team-season, subject to an explicit team invitation suspension. Club/Super Administrator access inherits the appropriate team authority. Issuing an invitation does not change the issuer's memberships or create Player/staff access.

The invitation URL carries an opaque, single-use capability in its fragment. Its team and season are verified by the service; the club/team names on the joining screen come from the current stored configuration. Share and QR use the exact scoped URL. QR generation runs locally with `qrcode-generator`; no external QR provider receives the link. Email opens the user's email app with a prepared message for the user to review and send. This does not configure an SMTP provider or claim email delivery.

A new spectator chooses a username and four-digit PIN after reviewing a current invitation. Repeated digits, ascending/descending sequences including wraparound, repeated pairs and selected commonly guessed codes are rejected. Credentials are individually salted and derived with scrypt; plaintext PINs and raw session tokens are not persisted. Five failed attempts lock the username for 15 minutes across network addresses. HTTP address limits and bounded concurrent derivations supplement this persisted lock. Invitation requests normally allow five per address per hour. An explicitly isolated, nonproduction review raises only that request allowance to 30 because the review tunnel presents one shared address; PIN authentication and username lockout limits stay unchanged. A correct PIN during lockout does not unlock the account. Sessions are held in service memory for at most six hours and are revoked by sign-out or service restart. Device storage and the match journal do not retain PINs or session tokens.

Registration consumes the possessed invitation and updates the invitation mirror, community grant and spectator membership atomically. PIN identities are always unclassified, unverified for adult authority and `strongAuth: false`. Existing accounts can sign in and accept another team invitation without changing their PIN. The existing named Club/Team selector then selects among their current grants. Existing preauthorised review accounts retain their existing login details; they are separate from this invitation registration flow.

Spectators may choose the whole team or a current player to support. The service returns only player identifiers and display names in this connection selector, after checking a current spectator grant. The account's choice is an own-only community preference; it grants no Player identity, attendance, minutes, statistics or other private records. Foreign player identifiers are rejected by the connected endpoint. A revoked grant closes the selector and invitation issuance.

An unauthenticated visitor can choose a listed Club/Team and request either Player or Spectator access. The request stores a bounded name and contact email for review by that team's staff. Submission creates neither an account nor a membership. Spectator requests are fulfilled by issuing a spectator invitation; Player requests require staff to identify the correct player profile and follow the existing player activation journey. Staff may mark a request handled or decline it. Request contact details are unavailable to Players, Spectators and unrelated team managers. Club/team directory responses contain metadata only.

## Verification

- Three shared PIN-rule tests passed, including leading-zero codes and rejected patterns.
- Eight shared invitation tests passed after replacing the obsolete spectator-denial expectation with scoped invitation creation, unchanged memberships/grants and revoked-grant rejection.
- Seven actual SQLite service tests passed, including the separate normal/review request-limit boundaries: atomic account/grant creation, weak-PIN refusal without consuming the invitation, persisted lockout/restart, spectator invitation authority, private invite requests and disabled/expired sessions.
- Four actual PostgreSQL tests passed using separate non-owner, non-bypass API/worker roles. They verify atomic registration, denied direct credential reads and worker helper access, persisted lockout, own following and revoked/foreign scope denial, and private request review.
- Three actual HTTP journeys passed: registration/PIN sign-in/logout, invitation acceptance into an existing account, and unauthenticated requests with staff review. They include role-header forgery rejection, minimal connection DTOs and foreign-player rejection.
- Existing demo-session client tests passed with PIN-aware logout routing. Focused UI lint passed with zero errors or warnings.

The PostgreSQL feature uses migration 024 and API-only helper grants. Credentials have no direct runtime table access. Invitation requests have staff-scoped row security. The only new spectator document writes support existing canonical invitation issuance/reissue; ordinary community preference writes retain the prior own-account command boundary.

These are generated-data review checks. A camera scan of the rendered QR, native email/share sheets, native keyboards and physical-device lifecycle behaviour still require device testing. Q05/Q09/Q10 privacy, retention and support decisions remain pending; this feature does not approve them or configure a paid service.
